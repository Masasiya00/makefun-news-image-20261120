# Friday 20 November 2026: The World in One Day

One image summing up the world's news of Friday 20 November 2026:
45 stories across 7 regions — Russia's 400-drone barrage on Kyiv,
Cyclone Akvan's landfall on India's east coast, the first three-way
Trump–Xi–Putin meeting in Shenzhen, and 42 more, every one of them
first reported on 20 November.

The page is a newspaper front page, drawn in code with Python and
Pillow. A navy masthead crowns it; underneath, a strip of six numbers,
a bar splitting the stories by region, three big hero pictures, ten
medium story cards each carrying a sourcing tag, a world map with
numbered pins, a "32 MORE STORIES" list, and a DATE CHECK box listing
what was left out on purpose. Every headline stays sharp and readable
at full size. Thirteen pictures sit on the page — all AI-generated
illustrations in a dramatic, photojournalistic style, artwork rather
than photographs — each made for this entry and captioned with its own
story. The Van Gogh card is the exception: a flat picture drawn in code.

## Reading the page

At the very top is the masthead — title, date, and the count of
45 stories across 7 regions. Just below it, the stats strip picks out
the six numbers that define the day: 400+ drones in the Kyiv barrage,
110 missiles fired at Kyiv, the $190.4 million record Van Gogh sale,
2 missiles landing in Poland, the $81 billion Anthropic IPO, and
Nvidia falling 14% on an SEC probe. The region bar underneath shows
how the 45 stories split across the world.

Next come the three hero stories, the biggest news of the day, each in
its own colour with its own picture: Kyiv in red, Akvan in teal,
Shenzhen in orange. Under them, ten medium cards carry the next ten
stories, each marked with a tag for how solid its sourcing is:
CONFIRMED is two or more outlets, OFFICIAL is on the record,
REPORTED means a single outlet, and CLAIM means one side's account.
The map pins stories 1–13 to the places they happened, while the
"32 MORE STORIES" list carries the rest, numbered 14–45. The DATE
CHECK box at the bottom names the big 19 November stories left out on
purpose, so no one mistakes them for 20 November news.

## How the entry works

`main.ts` (Node 22, standard library only) copies `entry.png` into
`/output/entry.png`. It needs no network and writes the same bytes on
every run. Before writing, it checks the file: it must exist, it must
be a PNG, and it must be at most 1 MiB. After writing, it reads the
copy back and compares it byte for byte, so a bad write fails loudly.

The picture itself comes from a Python Pillow script that lives
outside this repository. It lays out the masthead, the stats strip, the
region bar, the hero cards, the medium cards, the map, the "more" list
and the DATE CHECK box, pastes the thirteen illustrations into their
frames, and saves a 1024x1024 PNG. The script was written by hand for
this entry and is deterministic: the same inputs always produce the
same bytes.

## Running it

```sh
mkdir -p out && MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=out node main.ts
```

That leaves `out/entry.png` byte-identical to `entry.png`.
